# Sources for the Montana broadleaf tree key

Checked 2026-10-16. MTFG = Montana Field Guide (Montana Natural Heritage
Program and Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks), https://fieldguide.mt.gov; USDA =
USDA PLANTS, https://plants.usda.gov; VT = Virginia Tech Dendrology fact
sheets, https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu, used for characters MTFG does not give
(leaf-stalk shape, buds).

Excluded:

- Common hackberry, *Celtis occidentalis*: MTFG gives its origin in Montana
  as "Undetermined Origin" (state rank SNR; Big Horn and Custer counties),
  https://fieldguide.mt.gov/speciesDetail.aspx?elcode=PDULM01040. USDA lists
  it as native to the lower 48 (https://plants.usda.gov/home/plantProfile?symbol=CEOC).
  Not both native in Montana, so left out.
- American elm (*Ulmus americana*): not native to Montana.

## Characters used, by couplet

1. Compound vs. simple leaves. Box elder: "Leaves 3- or (rarely) 5-foliolate" (MTFG PDACE01090); green ash: "odd-pinnate leaves ... 5 to 7 leaflets" (MTFG PDOLE040D0); Rocky Mountain maple is simple in the northern part of its range (VT ID=161, https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu/dendrology/syllabus/factsheet.cfm?ID=161).
2. Box elder: leaflets "coarsely few-toothed", 3 (rarely 5) (MTFG); green ash: 5 to 7 leaflets, "serrate margins" (MTFG). Both opposite: green ash "oppositely arranged" (MTFG); box elder (VT ID=3, https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu/dendrology/syllabus/factsheet.cfm?ID=3).
3. Rocky Mountain maple: "opposite", "3-5 serrated lobes" (VT ID=161); "3- to 5-lobed, doubly serrate; petioles red" (MTFG). All remaining species are alternate (MTFG and VT leaf descriptions).
4. Bur oak: "pinnately lobed, the lobes rounded"; "acorn" (MTFG PDFAG05190).
5. Narrow leaves: peachleaf willow "lanceolate, long-acuminate", 3–8 cm (MTFG PDSAL02030); narrowleaf cottonwood "lanceolate ... 2.5 or more times as long as wide, 4–11 cm" (MTFG PDSAL01020). Broad leaves: aspen "broadly ovate to orbicular" (MTFG); plains cottonwood "deltoid, ca. as long as wide" (MTFG); black cottonwood "broadly lance-shaped with truncate bases, 5–15 cm" (MTFG); birches 2–7 cm (MTFG).
6. Willow buds "covered by a single, cap-like scale" / "covered by one bud scale" (VT lab notes for *Salix*, https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu/dendrology/lab1.cfm?t1=2, and VT ID=86); peachleaf willow "glaucous beneath" (MTFG). Narrowleaf cottonwood buds "long (1 inch), curved, resinous and fragrant" (VT ID=898, https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu/dendrology/syllabus/factsheet.cfm?ID=898).
7. Flattened leaf stalks: aspen "petiole is flattened" (VT ID=160); plains cottonwood "petiole is flattened" (VT ID=64). Black cottonwood "petiole ... most often round but may be flattened" (VT ID=235); birch leaf stalks are not flattened.
8. Aspen: "broadly ovate to orbicular ... 2–8 cm" (MTFG), "fine toothed margin" (VT ID=160), "bark smooth, pale" (MTFG). Plains cottonwood: "deltoid ... margins with teeth at least 1 mm deep" (MTFG), 5–10 cm.
9. Black cottonwood: 5–15 cm, "pale below" (MTFG), "silvery white below with rusty smears of resin" (VT ID=235). Birches: blades 2–7 cm with "sharp-dentate margins", and no silvery or resin-stained underside is described for either birch (MTFG PDBET02060, PDBET020B0).
10. Paper birch: bark "white and peeling when older" (MTFG); water birch: "Gray to brown bark, only slightly peeling", "Small tree or large shrub to ca. 8 m tall", "Margins of streams and wetlands" (MTFG).
